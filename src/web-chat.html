Getting a bigger model
  • Describe the difficulty, not the task. Jev is picking the cheapest model that can finish the job, so "transcribe this PDF" reads as mechanical and lands on Haiku. "Transcribe it preserving table structure exactly — errors are costly" reads as judgement, and routes up.
  • Say when you do not know the cause. A bug with an unknown cause is a diagnosis problem and routes high. A bug you have already diagnosed is just an edit.
  • Name the blast radius. Auth, security, concurrency, data migration and anything touching money are treated as high-stakes on their own.
  • Ask for a decision. Weighing a trade-off and committing to one answer routes higher than producing text about the options.
  • Length words do not move it. "Briefly" and "in detail" describe the shape of the reply, not how hard it is to work out.
  • Every turn is routed on its own. If a follow-up is the hard part, say what makes it hard — the previous turn's tier does not carry over.
